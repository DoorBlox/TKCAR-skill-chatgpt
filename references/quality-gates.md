# Quality gates, adversarial review, and recurring failure repairs

Run this audit before declaring a concept ready for a decision, MM handoff, or public launch. For small requests, select the relevant tests rather than applying everything.

## Gate 0 — Source and authority integrity

- Are the user's requirements preserved accurately, including their actual uncertainty?
- Does each “mandatory” claim have a known authorizing source?
- Were inferred motives and private opinions kept out of public team documents?
- Are old versions or superseded preferences accidentally presented as current?
- Are any media references, linguistic roots, or factual claims invented?

**Stop/repair:** Separate fact, report, interpretation, and proposed change.

## Gate 1 — Distinctive creative core

**Genre-substitution test:** Replace the thematic genre word with a synonym. Does anything else change? If not, the concept may be generic.

**Five-second test:** Can the concept be described as a specific visual subject plus meaningful tension/action without jargon?

**De-branding test:** If the name/logo disappeared, would the artwork still be recognizable as the event's own identity?

**Failure repair:** Introduce a specific world mechanism, relationship, signature subject, institution, or transformative visual behavior. Do not merely add a more poetic title.

## Gate 2 — Boundaries and art direction

**Infinity test:** Is vastness actually a problem, or is the world already confined to a small set of relevant nodes? Avoid confusing background scale with concept scope.

**Asset-source test:** For eight representative deliverables, identify *where* each source of visual variation comes from.

**Hierarchy test:** Could a generic prop, secondary destination, or background visually overwhelm the intended central focus?

**Failure repair:** Clarify primary/secondary/environmental layers, deepen central anchors, and set inclusion/exclusion rules with rationales.

## Gate 3 — Story-to-world causality

**Remove-and-replace test:** Delete a core artifact, protagonist, location, or title metaphor. Does the story/world lose a meaningful function?

**Why-this-place test:** Why does the protagonist need to encounter each field? Would visiting a random alternative serve the same story?

**Functional mismatch test:** Does a structure serve a role natural to its identity? E.g., transport vessel as battle arena needs rationale.

**Ethical mechanic test:** Does the hero do something incongruent with the event's moral framing (e.g., take valuable resources from successful societies without consent)?

**Resolution test:** Does the climax follow from preceding changes, or does an unexplained fifth object steal attention?

**Failure repair:** Change causal mechanics and roles before adding explanatory prose. Distinguish **visual focus** and **story destination** rather than forcing them to be the same.

## Gate 4 — Multi-field identity and coherence

**Color-off test:** Do categories remain identifiable in grayscale?

**Franchise-collage test:** Do the four (or N) fields still belong to one world, or do they look like four unrelated moodboards?

**Purpose test:** Does each area's shape and architecture arise from what that field does, or from arbitrary decoration/stereotypes?

**Name-swap test:** Could the field labels be randomly exchanged without anyone noticing?

**Failure repair:** Shared design DNA plus locally unique architectural functions, silhouettes, motifs, movement, and atmosphere.

## Gate 5 — Execution reality

**MM echo test:** Can MM restate the intended hierarchy and world logic in their own words? If not, the brief is underdefined.

**Eight-assets test:** Can key visual, field graphic, carousel, animation, stage, signage, merch, and decor all be designed without violating the art direction?

**Scale test:** Does a huge lore structure simplify into a print-safe, stage-safe, labor-feasible visual treatment?

**Continuity test:** Can many MM contributors independently extend the design system?

**Feasibility honesty:** Which claims have been checked with MM, suppliers, logistics, venue, or budget? Don't assert “easy to make” without confirmation.

**Failure repair:** Produce 2–3 prototyped applications and request expert feedback. Reassess the concept if its defining assets cannot scale.

## Gate 6 — Audience and communication

**Plain-language test:** Can a 14–18-year-old participant (or actual intended audience) understand the invitation without an English literature lecture?

**CTA test:** Does the tagline ask the audience to *do* something when a call to action is requested?

**Thesaurus test:** Are ornate words masking weak meaning?

**Keyword-trap test:** Do all tagline proposals mechanically recycle title nouns in one identical sentence structure?

**Identity test:** Does the event title sound original, pronounceable, and suited to context rather than a synthetic phrase generator?

**Failure repair:** Rebuild the message around participant motive, stakes, action, and spoken rhythm. Vary rhetorical architecture, not synonyms.

## Gate 7 — Team adoption

**Stakeholder clarity:** Has a genuine decision owner accepted the direction, or merely expressed enthusiasm?

**Constructive dispute:** Have defenders' valid arguments been acknowledged? Are remaining objections accurately narrowed?

**Handoff ownership:** Can TKCAR identify what it owns and what MM may reinterpret?

**Change control:** Are provisional choices, owners, next tests, and lock dates/milestones visible?

**Failure repair:** Document decision and unresolved risks; run a short cross-functional check rather than assuming silence is agreement.

## Failure library: diagnose the mechanism, not the symptom

| Symptom | Likely mechanism | Best repair |
|---|---|---|
| Beautiful aesthetic, forgettable identity | Genre imagery without a unique premise | Invent a signature relationship or rule |
| Everything “matches the theme” but feels inconsistent | No shared visual grammar | Specify stable shape/material/mood system |
| Too much background, main subjects disappear | Weak hierarchy | Strengthen focal anchors and reduce generic scene clutter |
| Four areas look the same | Only color-based differences | Add function-based architecture and silhouette |
| Four areas look unrelated | Four separate franchise references | Set shared DNA and translate reference *properties* |
| Story reads like checkpoint collection | Categories inserted after narrative | Reassign meaningful causal roles |
| Poetic title seems detached | Post hoc symbolism | Connect metaphor to actual progression and graphic behavior |
| Mascot is cute but generic | Species/cuteness chosen before role | Start from personality, action, silhouette, story purpose |
| Mascot looks like last year | Reused anatomy/accessory formula | Change body dynamics and expression strategy |
| Taglines all sound alike | Locked to lore keywords and symmetrical templates | Explore different audience actions and sentence patterns |
| Seniors respond warmly, but team remains split | Courteous discussion mistaken for agreement | Get explicit decision status and next validation |
| Team debates forever | No comparative rubric or freeze point | Shortlist comparable options and select conditionally |
| A preferred idea “wins” every criterion | Confirmation bias or unequal elaboration | Write its best objection and a counterexample |

## Exit status

Assign one of these labels rather than an unjustified claim of perfection:

- **Exploratory:** Promising premise, constraints/viability still unclear.
- **Concept-ready:** Coherent world and narrative, ready for MM and stakeholder tests.
- **MM-reviewed:** Designers have provided feedback; important visual assumptions tested.
- **Production-ready:** Constraints signed off, key design system accepted, risky implementations prototyped.

Never promote a status without the evidence it requires.
