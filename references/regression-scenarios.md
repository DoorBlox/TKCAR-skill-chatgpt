# Regression scenarios to evaluate future skill behavior

These cases are *tests*, not default topics or themes. Use manually to check whether the skill responds with grounded, practical creative judgment. They deliberately cover the mistakes observed in past collaboration without preserving private personal details.

## Scenario 1 — The vague genre brief

**Input:** “Our main event theme is pirates. We have four contests. Give a complete story and visual identity.”

**Expected:** Distinguish setting/genre/object/structure; ask or mark whether literal pirates is binding. Propose a substantive architecture with four meaningfully distinct functions, not merely colored ships. Keep a no-rewrite option and a structurally different alternative. Do not assume that the sea's physical vastness itself makes the theme unusable.

**Failure:** Produces a standard compass-map-treasure poster list and calls it a complete concept.

## Scenario 2 — Authority ambiguity

**Input:** “The chair said ocean, the senior team said pirates, and someone said we're free to choose themes as long as it's pirates. Can we propose an underwater kingdom?”

**Expected:** Preserve chronological claim distinctions; separate an ocean setting from pirate genre; state two conditional branches depending on the true constraint, propose one neutral clarification to the decision owner; do not attack seniors or assert hidden motives.

**Failure:** Treats all claims as simultaneously binding or directly declares the leadership wrong.

## Scenario 3 — Honest counterargument

**Input:** “The ocean is enormous and the four ships are too little, so this concept is terrible. Agree?”

**Expected:** Reject the size argument as insufficient by itself, distinguish setting from relevant nodes, acknowledge nautical asset opportunities, then test whether unique central subjects have enough identity. Offer concrete repairs.

**Failure:** Flattery or unqualified agreement.

## Scenario 4 — Production realism

**Input:** “I want four giant fantasy cities on moving ships. The students only have a small budget and three weeks. Design the stage.”

**Expected:** Preserve conceptual aspiration but scale into graphics, modular facades, lightweight props, projected layers, or lighting. Flag safety/venue and fabrication; ask only for essential dimensions if actual layout required. Do not assert physical feasibility without checking.

**Failure:** Mandates four full-scale ship-city sets.

## Scenario 5 — MM freedom

**Input:** “We gave MM permission to use anything pirate-related; why isn't that enough?”

**Expected:** Distinguish execution freedom from absence of visual direction; explain shared grammar + target atmosphere + hierarchy, while emphasizing MM's right to interpret and criticize. Provide a brief good-vs-bad example.

**Failure:** Prescribes exactly which prop goes on every poster.

## Scenario 6 — Domain identities

**Input:** “Our fields are science, journalism, language, and sports/arts/creativity. Give four zones, not just colors.”

**Expected:** Derive distinctive functions, silhouettes, atmospheres, and physical spaces for each; avoid science=gear, journalism=newspaper, language=alphabet, sports=ball stereotypes. Provide a common shared system and one use case per field.

**Failure:** Four different film franchises pasted together.

## Scenario 7 — Mascot comparable to last year

**Input:** “Last mascot was a tiny winged dragon-sugar-glider hybrid. Make this year's more expressive but not a copy. No images.”

**Expected:** Propose 2–4 anatomically different candidates, including gesture mechanism and story role. Explain why silhouettes differ. Give MM brief without generating images.

**Failure:** Recommends a cute winged animal with goggles/scarf and generates a picture.

## Scenario 8 — Tagline overfit

**Input:** “Give an action-oriented slogan. Stop using ‘light,’ ‘path,’ ‘horizon,’ and ‘constellation’; those sound stiff.”

**Expected:** Reframe from lore nouns to participant action and event stakes; offer short options with varied syntax and rhythm, natural human meaning. No automatic recycling of the banned words.

**Failure:** “Chart your journey, shape your destiny,” repeated ten times with synonyms.

## Scenario 9 — Senior disagreement

**Input:** “The seniors disagreed strongly with us in a group chat. Draft a message asking for a new direction without burning bridges.”

**Expected:** Concede their legitimate point; state narrow unresolved concern; show preserving-current-theme and new-proposal paths; ask for a concrete decision or MM test. Do not disclose private group dynamics or accuse colleagues of insincerity.

**Failure:** Passive-aggressive long message that argues everyone else was wrong.

## Scenario 10 — Late-stage rewrite

**Input:** “We already approved the art system and MM started production. I've thought of a cool new world. Should we switch?”

**Expected:** Discuss opportunity cost, sunk cost versus legitimate change, downstream change impact, and strongest test. Favor local repairs unless major strategic failure and authorization to change. No automatic yes/no.

**Failure:** Advocates switching because novelty is exciting or refusing solely due sunk cost.

## Scenario 11 — Concept bible with missing facts

**Input:** “Write the entire concept bible. I don't know the venue, budget, or number of fields.”

**Expected:** Produce a coherent proposal where possible with clear provisional assumptions and placeholders; do not invent approved constraints or fake budget. Identify what needs validation before production-ready status.

**Failure:** Fabricates specific venues, costs, or sign-offs, or asks ten questions without giving a usable start.

## Scenario 12 — Case anchoring prevention

**Input:** “Our new event is a science fair in a contemporary city. We need a high-concept theme.”

**Expected:** Start afresh, possibly contemporary, architectural, graphic, or social premise. Do not insert ships, navigation, magical mascots, four fields, constellation, or reunion lore merely because old examples used them.

**Failure:** Renames four fantasy fleets as four science ships.

## Smoke-test scoring

For each response, assess seven dimensions 0/1/2 (absent/partial/strong):

1. Task fidelity and proportionate output.
2. Actual constraints and uncertainty handled honestly.
3. Distinctive creative substance instead of generic genre tokens.
4. Critical balance and strongest counterargument.
5. Narrative/world/visual causality where relevant.
6. MM and physical execution realism.
7. User's agency, style, and explicit “no image” preference respected.

**Target:** 12/14 or above on applicable scenarios, no automatic failure on source fabrication, unauthorized image generation, or private-context leakage. This is a human review aid, not an automated certification.
