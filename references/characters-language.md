# Mascots, character systems, themes, and audience-facing language

## 1. Mascot brief: why character first, species second

Begin with the job this character must do:

- **Narrative function:** guide, challenger, witness, messenger, companion, guardian, mischief-maker, mediator, or symbol of an event value.
- **Relationship to participants:** welcoming, aspirational, funny, brave, curious, reassuring, charismatic.
- **Visual communication tasks:** stickers, stage banners, category reactions, social posts, standees, merch, animated loops.
- **Emotional range:** can it visibly celebrate, think, worry, fail, recover, encourage, and learn?

Only then explore candidate anatomies. Animal choice should support expression and lore, not be arbitrary theming.

## 2. Character anatomy and expressiveness

Evaluate:

- **Silhouette:** distinctive ears/horns/fins/tail/wing/shell/sail/antenna; still readable at 32–64 px.
- **Face:** eyes, brow, cheek, mouth or alternative expression mechanism.
- **Gestures:** hands, paws, wings, tail, posture, body squash/stretch.
- **Movement:** walk, climb, float, glide, leap, swim, roll, bounce; fits the world.
- **Signature attachment:** one memorable accessory or organic marking with narrative purpose.
- **Craft practicality:** can a simple 2D vector, plush, or cutout preserve identity?
- **Independence:** doesn't look like last year's mascot with altered colors and glasses.

Hybrids can combine an expressive base animal and one fantasy element. Avoid stitching many unrelated features together. Don't automatically choose wings/scarves/goggles when a previous mascot already used them.

## 3. Mascot idea card

For 2–4 distinct routes, provide:

**Archetype** → **biological/fantasy inspiration** → **three unmistakable traits** → **movement** → **personality flaw/strength** → **story role** → **small-scale execution concern**.

Useful contrast: a wise observant creature, an impulsive agile creature, a transforming shape creature; do not make all three “small cute guardian with sparkling eyes.”

Do not overdefine exact anatomical forms when the user wants MM to develop them. A brief should be *specific enough to orient but open enough to design*.

## 4. Writing an MM-facing mascot brief

Use one paragraph of 90–180 words, ideally covering size/shape, expressive qualities, signature anatomy, story role, and freedom. Example with placeholder identity:

> [Name] merupakan makhluk fiksi berukuran [scale] dengan karakter [personality]. Bentuknya mengambil inspirasi gerak [animal/physical property] dan elemen fantasi [one functional feature], sehingga dapat [movement]. Ciri utamanya adalah [silhouette feature] dan [single signature detail], yang berkaitan dengan perannya sebagai [story role]. Desain perlu mendukung ekspresi [three contrasting emotions] serta penggunaan pada stiker, ilustrasi, dan dekorasi. MM bebas mengembangkan proporsi dan detail selama siluet, kepribadian, dan hubungan dengan dunia acara tetap terbaca.

The example is a format, not a mascot recommendation.

## 5. Names: semantic fit versus pronounceability

Generate 6–12 options *when requested*, grouped by distinct naming strategies:

- Meaning-based: relevant term or traceable root.
- Phonetic/character-based: memorable sounds that match personality.
- Fictional coined form: original-sounding, simple to pronounce.
- World-derived: name follows the setting's plausible linguistic convention.

Assess **pronunciation in Indonesian and English (as relevant), memorability, character fit, distinction, and linguistic honesty**. Do not invent false etymologies for pretty names. Verify claims about a language/constellation/myth when factual accuracy matters. Also avoid accidental strong associations with famous existing characters.

## 6. Title, tagline, and slogan are different jobs

- **Theme title:** names the world/premise and signals its aesthetic identity.
- **Philosophy sentence:** explains what the theme means to the event.
- **Tagline:** gives the audience a compelling thought, promise, or invitation.
- **Call-to-action tagline:** explicitly urges a meaningful action (or two), with a clear sense of agency.
- **Internal creative motto:** helps the team design consistently; need not appear publicly.

Do not demand that all of these repeat the same five lore nouns. A good CTA can live outside the fictional vocabulary while supporting its underlying motivation.

## 7. Tagline generation system: diversify rhetorical moves

Before generating words, identify the desired audience action: dare, explore, make, compete, discover, connect, think, celebrate, transform, take responsibility, etc. Consider at least **four distinct moves**, not ten synonym pairs:

1. **Challenge/dares:** invite a step into uncertainty.
2. **Identity/development:** invite growth or a new self-understanding.
3. **Contribution/collaboration:** urge creation of something shared.
4. **Discovery/curiosity:** invite testing assumptions or learning.
5. **Competitive energy:** urge demonstration of skill or courage.
6. **Narrative invitation:** answer a call, enter a world, take part in change.

For shortlist candidates, explain *what participant action* each invokes, not simply the lore word it echoes. Include a few with fresh syntax: question, single imperative, contrast, short phrase, one longer line. Avoid 20 sentences of identical “Verb your X. Verb the Y.”

## 8. Tagline checks

Reject or revise if:

- It could fit any generic graduation poster without a meaningful twist.
- The action is unclear or purely descriptive.
- It sounds like a collection of thesaurus words.
- It leans too heavily on the title's literal nouns.
- It is grammatically awkward or unnatural when spoken aloud.
- It promises an outcome the event cannot plausibly deliver.
- It sounds unintentionally aggressive, elitist, exclusionary, or culturally unsuitable.
- It doesn't survive a 3-second poster reading or an MC reading it live.

Check *spoken cadence* and whether Indonesian-language interpretation is coherent. A meaningful English line should still have a plain-language rationale, not an elaborate retrospective metaphor.

## 9. How to respond when the user says “too stiff / too AI / too constrained by keywords”

Do not simply replace “course” with “journey” and “light” with “spark.” State the failed approach briefly, then **change the underlying rhetorical strategy**: participant motivations, energetic verbs, human emotion, active tension, local idiom, varying cadence and length. Propose a distinct shortlist with one line of rationale each. Avoid defensiveness and repetition.

## 10. Mascot/naming evaluation mini-rubric

Rate 1–5 with short notes:

- **Recognizability:** silhouette and sound distinctive?
- **Concept fit:** genuine function, not forced etymology?
- **Expressiveness:** enough poses and emotions?
- **Feasibility:** easy to reproduce across formats?
- **Adaptability:** good in teaser, print, digital, physical?
- **Originality:** not a thin reskin of last year or a famous IP?

Names, mascots, and taglines remain provisional until the appropriate team approves them.
