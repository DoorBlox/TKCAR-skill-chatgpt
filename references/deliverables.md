# Output contracts and reusable templates

Choose a format proportional to the request. Prefer structured thinking behind the scenes; don't drown users in rigid headings or tables unless they ask for a working document.

## A. Five-minute concept critique

**Bottom line:** one paragraph assessing actual strength and weakness.

**What works:** 2–3 evidence-backed strengths.

**Actual problem:** identify the *most consequential* unclarified or weak design decision. Distinguish from personal aesthetic preference.

**Strongest defense:** fair argument a supporter would make.

**Repairs:** two alternatives—one that preserves the existing idea, one that changes its structure.

**Test:** one low-cost evidence-producing step (MM review, asset prototype, compare options, clarified requirement).

Avoid “this is perfect” and “scrap everything” unless compellingly justified.

## B. Equal-depth concept shortlist

For each of ~3 options:

1. **Working title (optional).**
2. **Premise:** two sentences maximum.
3. **Central motif:** distinct, visible, not just an abstract value.
4. **World/boundaries:** central scope and exclusions.
5. **Fields:** how required categories function.
6. **Experience/story:** one cause-effect arc or event journey.
7. **Visual richness:** 5–8 unique asset sources.
8. **MM/physical execution:** likely bottleneck.
9. **Differentiator:** why choose this instead of other options.

Then compare, recommend conditionally, and propose one falsification test for the chosen route.

## C. One-page concept pitch

**Event + working concept**

**One-line premise:** The distinctive central idea.

**What people see:** A 30-second image-in-words, foreground to background.

**Why it belongs to this event:** Audience, program, values, category connection.

**World logic:** Primary anchors, setting, boundaries, rules.

**Story in 5 beats:** Before / change / journey / encounter / resolution.

**Field differentiation:** Short line for each required area; do not assume four.

**Visual language:** Artstyle + materials + shape + palette logic + mood.

**Production opportunity:** 3 digital and 2 physical uses.

**Risks and open decisions:** What MM/organizers must validate.

## D. Full concept bible (only for explicit comprehensive requests)

1. Executive creative brief, state of approval, constraints ledger.
2. Core concept and significance to the event.
3. World overview and boundaries with 4–8 rules.
4. Meaning/symbolism *tied to story and design*.
5. Full causal storyline and short public-facing synopsis.
6. Character roles, including mascot brief if requested.
7. Hero art direction: composition, form, materials, graphic treatment, palette hierarchy, lighting.
8. Every category's identity sheet: purpose, plot role, structure, geometry, motifs, color, differentiator, world linkage.
9. Reference packet: target properties, search queries, what to extract, what to avoid.
10. Digital campaign progression and asset matrix.
11. Stage/decor and physical implementation principles.
12. Title/tagline directions if requested, with meaning and audience-facing rationale.
13. MM feedback questions, decision log, risks, version status.
14. QA: what was stress-tested and what remains speculative.

**Avoid:** repetitive lore paragraphs, a giant random prop list, unverified production claims, arbitrary maps/specs, and old-event names accidentally inserted as “working” ones.

## E. Multimedia handoff sheet

| Field | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Visual North Star | One sentence describing the intended visual experience |
| Hero Anchor | What should dominate the most important compositions |
| Must Keep | 3–6 non-negotiable world/style rules with rationale |
| Open to MM | Decisions the designers can adapt and own |
| Must Avoid | Visual motifs that would break concept (not arbitrary dislikes) |
| Shared DNA | Shape, material, palette, light, typography, graphic patterns |
| Category Differences | Meaningful distinctions beyond accent colors |
| Campaign Story | How visuals change from teaser to finale, if relevant |
| References | Specific scenes/works + **exact property to borrow** |
| Stress Assets | 3 high-risk prototypes to test before broad rollout |
| Risks | Ambiguities and physical/technical constraints requiring input |
| Approval | Which parts are approved versus provisional; reviewer/owner |

MM should be encouraged to push back. The sheet is a starting contract for collaboration, not a final design specification in disguise.

## F. Workshop / WhatsApp disagreement reply

Draft a realistic message that:

- Opens by recognizing a valid clarification.
- States the unresolved concern without repeating every prior argument.
- Connects the concern to a real design or event consequence.
- Offers a preserving-the-theme repair and a compare-alternatives option.
- Asks for a concrete decision/meeting outcome rather than vaguely “what do you think?”

**Sample tone in Indonesian:**

> Okee aku nangkep kalau [setting] itu sebenarnya cuma background, jadi concern kita bukan harus menjelajahi semuanya. Yang masih pengen kita dalemin justru [central anchor] biar identitas tiap bidangnya kebaca dan MM punya arah yang konsisten. Bisa banget kita keep konsep ini sambil bikin identity sheet per bidang, tapi menurutku worth juga bandingin dengan satu alternatif yang dibangun sampai level detail yang sama. Nanti kita tes bareng MM supaya nggak cuma berdasarkan asumsi kita.

Replace bracketed parts with actual facts. Do not copy private remarks about colleagues or falsely imply consensus.

## G. Decision and experiment record

**Hypothesis:** “This world will sustain diverse category content while staying cohesive.”

**Experiment:** Request 3 small prototypes: hero visual, one category visual, one stage/decor layout.

**Success criteria:** identifiable focus, coherent style, distinct category, manageable labor/materials.

**Observed outcomes:** Record MM feedback and production constraints; don't fabricate.

**Decision:** Keep / refine / pivot / abandon, with short rationale and owner.

## H. Iterative conversation protocol

When the user changes a small choice (rename mascot, rewrite tagline), **preserve approved surrounding elements**. Do not reimagine the whole event unless asked. When user says “only words,” deliver text without visual-generation tools. When asked for an artifact, use the designated artifact workflow; do not arbitrarily create files.
