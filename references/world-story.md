# World architecture, narrative logic, and meaning

## 1. World concept canvas

Define these seven things in plain language *before* writing lyrical lore:

1. **One-sentence premise:** What is unusually true about this world/event?
2. **Signature anchor:** Which person, place, structure, object, or transformation is its most recognizable element?
3. **Scope:** Which parts of the potentially larger setting actually matter?
4. **Rules:** Two to five consistent constraints that determine what belongs.
5. **Diversity mechanism:** How do required competition fields become distinct, not merely recolored?
6. **Pressure or opportunity:** Why is action necessary or exciting?
7. **Resolution:** What meaningful state changes by the end?

Treat this as a design system for decisions, not a demand to explain every scientific law or kingdom's history.

## 2. Boundary definition is not geographical closure

An idea can take place in an infinite galaxy yet refer only to four stations. This can be well bounded. Conversely, a single house can be conceptually chaotic when random motifs are imported.

Write:

- **Primary zone:** areas/subjects of narrative and visual focus.
- **Functional background:** setting that gives context; no obligation to exhaustively populate it.
- **Optional peripherals:** expandable material if a new asset needs variety.
- **Out-of-scope vocabulary:** elements that could plausibly exist but would dilute the agreed identity.

Avoid arbitrary bans; explain how exclusions preserve meaning and coherence. Avoid making the background visually dominant if the intended focus is elsewhere.

## 3. Hierarchy: plot goal and visual identity can differ

A vehicle could represent a division visually while a destination resolves the plot. These roles can coexist. The issue is not multiple important elements per se, but *unclarified hierarchy*.

Use this hierarchy table when a dispute concerns “what is the real focus?”

| Element | Narrative role | Visual role | Symbolic role | Frequency/scale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Central anchor | [protagonist, stage, transformation...] | [hero key visual] | [event identity] | [high] |
| Background setting | [context] | [atmosphere] | [optional] | [moderate] |
| Categories | [encounters/institutions...] | [sub-brand families] | [fields] | [high in category graphics] |
| Destination/resolution | [plot end] | [final reveal or environment] | [shared goal] | [controlled] |

If two roles compete, choose a deliberate hierarchy and communicate it to MM. Do **not** insist that plot destination must always equal the main visual focus.

## 4. Role-logic audit

For each important element, answer:

- Why does it exist in the world?
- What function does it fulfill *before* the protagonist encounters it?
- Why must the protagonist interact with it?
- Why is this interaction a logical challenge, not merely a category checklist?
- What changes afterward?
- Can participants understand the gist without reading all the lore?

**Example of a mismatch:** A hero already travels on one ship but must “conquer four ships” solely because there are four contest fields. Ships are usually transportation; a conquest mechanic needs explicit fictional logic. Repair by giving vessels distinct societies/problems, making stations meaningful destinations, or changing what the four elements are. Never paper over with paragraphs of lore.

## 5. Causal narrative backbone

Use a five-beat sequence, adapting for non-fantasy events:

1. **Before:** Normal state and meaningful diversity.
2. **Disruption/opportunity:** What changes, breaks, or becomes possible?
3. **Call and journey:** What does the protagonist/participants need and why?
4. **Escalation through differences:** Distinct fields cause different consequences or insights.
5. **Transformation/payoff:** An ending follows *because* of earlier decisions, not because an unrelated beautiful object appears.

Each major beat should enable a visual scene, emotional shift, event touchpoint, or category identity. If a lore detail supports none, consider removing it.

## 6. Motivations and ethics

Question implications of character actions: A hero reclaiming four artifacts from thriving societies can read as stealing their most valued possessions. Do not solve this by calling the hero a “good pirate.” Recast the mechanic: exchange, voluntary contribution, co-creation, mutually beneficial alliance, earned permission, or discovery of something no group loses.

Avoid automatic good/evil stereotyping and avoid assuming competition is inherently conquest. Events can also center on collaboration, discovery, cultural exchange, mastery, resilience, or self-expression.

## 7. Narrative-to-brand translation

Write a **story-to-asset map**:

| Narrative function | Potential visual expression | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Initial separation | Disconnected compositions and negative space | Teaser phase |
| First discovery | Distinct motif gains clarity | Category reveal |
| Progression | Symbols connect/change with a consistent rule | Countdown/motion |
| Final synthesis | Existing motifs form a recognizable integrated form | Main reveal/stage |

Do not rely on a single unexplained “magic crystal” or other generic symbol unless it has causal and visual necessity.

## 8. Metaphor integration without keyword captivity

A title, philosophy, and graphic language should reinforce each other, but need not be literal equivalents. For example, a music metaphor can manifest through multiple visual voices and complementary rhythms rather than instruments everywhere. A journey metaphor can manifest through decisions and change, not compulsively through maps.

Use three tests:

- **Removal:** If a symbol disappears, does the concept lose anything?
- **Swap:** If we replace a title word, does anything meaningful change in narrative/design? If not, the word may be ornamental.
- **Overliteralization:** Would forcing a literal depiction make the world stranger or less usable than a structural/metaphorical interpretation?

Use fewer strong symbols rather than many weak ones. Philosophy is a *design constraint with a reason*, not a post hoc essay explaining arbitrary decorations.

## 9. Category architecture template

Each required field, region, or faction gets:

- **Human purpose:** what its participants or inhabitants do and value.
- **Narrative challenge/role:** how it changes the story.
- **Architecture/space:** which facilities naturally exist there.
- **Visual geometry:** silhouette/shape logic.
- **Signature motifs and systems:** functional, not clipart.
- **Atmosphere and motion:** the feeling and activity there.
- **Accent palette:** subordinate to shared art direction.
- **Cross-world link:** what unmistakably makes it part of the same event.
- **Hero use case:** one key visual and one physical application.

Avoid reductive mappings such as “language = letters,” “science = gears,” “sports = balls.” Start from *purpose and action*, then derive forms.

## 10. Make worlds legible at several depths

- **5 seconds:** One unmistakable image/premise.
- **30 seconds:** A participant can understand the four identities and central invitation.
- **2 minutes:** A teammate can explain the causal story.
- **Full brief:** MM/production can infer consistent details and campaign progression.

Complexity that only works after reading ten pages is not yet a usable event identity.
