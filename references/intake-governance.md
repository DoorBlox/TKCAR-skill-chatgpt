# Intake, authority, and team dynamics

## 1. Brief intake without an interrogation

Extract available details from the conversation/files before asking. Ask the *one or two* missing facts most likely to alter the recommendation. Use this compact mental intake:

| Dimension | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Event job | What does the event do—competition, festival, recruitment, celebration? | Ensures the theme serves a real event |
| Audience | Who participates, in what age range, and from where? | Tone, accessibility, communication |
| Fields | Which disciplines, divisions, programs, or participant paths must be reflected? | Determines differentiation logic |
| Practical frame | Venues, production capacity, time, finances, approvals? | Prevents fantasy assets from being impossible to implement |
| Creative brief | What did organizers *actually* require? | Prevents imaginary restrictions |
| Design partners | Who translates the concept into media, decoration, stage, mascot? | Determines handoff depth |
| Status | Exploration, shortlist, approved concept, or active production? | Determines whether revision is still responsible |
| Decision owner | Who can reject, modify, or formally approve direction? | Avoids political ambiguity |

For early ideation, lack of a budget or exact audience count does not block ideation; use conditional assumptions and mark feasibility unconfirmed. For physical-installation proposals, budget/venue may become essential.

## 2. Constraint taxonomy

A recurring failure pattern: leadership provides a broad *setting*, someone proposes a genre, a supportive response is treated as formal approval, then later teammates call that genre compulsory. Prevent this by writing down exactly which layer is fixed:

- **Setting:** sea, sky, city, underground, outer space.
- **Subject/object:** boat, door, instrument, relic, building.
- **Genre/aesthetic:** pirates, noir, solarpunk, fairy tale, sci-fi.
- **Narrative:** quest, return, reunion, discovery, rescue.
- **Structure:** four fields, three days, one mascot, one central hub.
- **Brand identity:** logo system, existing slogan, school colors.

For each proposed requirement, record **literal wording**, **originator**, **when mentioned**, **scope**, **approval status**, and **interpretation still open**. Avoid declaring an interpretation binding unless confirmed by an authorized stakeholder.

Example decision ledger:

| Item | Classification | Evidence | Owner | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marine atmosphere | Confirmed request | Event brief quote | Chair | Preserve |
| Literal pirates | Proposed, not confirmed | Senior member's idea accepted enthusiastically | Creative lead | Clarify |
| Four areas | Mandatory | Competition structure | Program lead | Build four identity routes |
| Purple field accent | Preference | Moodboard discussion | MM | Test contrast |

These example contents are illustrative only; never carry them into the user's event.

## 3. Approval protocol

- **Idea suggested** does not mean **approved**.
- **Leadership likes it** does not necessarily mean **mandatory**.
- **A polite response** does not mean a colleague's substantive objections are resolved.
- **A group vote** may not override a formally imposed constraint.
- **A senior's interpretation** can be wrong without that senior acting in bad faith.

Whenever a constraint is disputed, recommend a neutral clarification, such as:

> Supaya eksplorasi kita nggak salah arah, aku mau samakan pemahaman dulu: yang fixed itu suasana/setting-nya, elemen utamanya, atau genre spesifiknya? Kalau boleh, kita catat juga bagian mana yang masih bisa kita usulkan alternatif.

Write the request as a shared coordination problem, not an accusation.

## 4. Managing disagreements with actual argument quality

Read longer chat histories chronologically, not as one monolithic debate. Locate:

1. What was proposed originally?
2. Which decision was genuinely made and by whom?
3. What changed, and when?
4. Which objection was answered?
5. Which concern remains unanswered?
6. Does the latest discussion supersede earlier assumptions?
7. What did external implementers (e.g., MM) actually say, versus what the team predicts they would say?

Separate **substantive claims** from **emotional tone**. A heated reply might contain a valid correction. Recognize it.

### Strong diplomatic critique pattern

- **Concede** an accurate point: “Benar, laut tidak harus dibahas seluruhnya; bisa jadi background.”
- **Narrow** the concern: “Yang belum kuat adalah identitas visual dari empat pusat utamanya.”
- **Explain the production consequence:** “Kalau hanya berbeda warna, asset campaign bisa repetitif.”
- **Offer two viable repairs:** “Perdalam tiap area atau coba struktur dunia alternatif.”
- **Invite evidence or collaboration:** “Kita tes bareng MM lewat tiga contoh output.”

Avoid assigning motives or using private allegations as public facts. An ally's support is useful for opening a conversation, but a proposal needs independent merit.

## 5. Meeting facilitation

For a 45–60 minute concept discussion, allocate approximately:

1. 5 min: state confirmed requirements and goal of this meeting.
2. 10 min: share comparable alternatives; no premature name debate.
3. 10 min: privately note strengths/risks by rubric before open discussion.
4. 10 min: discuss the two most consequential disagreements.
5. 10 min: MM/production reality check.
6. 5 min: decision, owner, unresolved facts, next checkpoint.

Adapt duration to the actual setting. Meetings may choose **a conditional direction** rather than final approval when a requirement is still unknown. Write down what could invalidate the direction.

## 6. Decision note format

Use a compact record:

- **Decision:** what was selected or rejected.
- **Why:** 2–4 criteria that influenced it.
- **What it does *not* mean:** prevent downstream overinterpretation.
- **Provisional elements:** names, palette, character details not yet approved.
- **Open risks:** assumption or feasibility to test.
- **Consult:** named divisions/decision owners (when known).
- **Next review:** event milestone, not invented deadlines.

## 7. Confidentiality and source discipline

If private chats were given as background, do not copy sensitive interpersonal remarks, names, or group dynamics into a handoff document intended for the entire committee. Summarize neutral lessons. When a particular participant's statement matters for accuracy, preserve their meaning and distinguish a direct quote from an inferred stance. Do not present unverified off-chat discussions as meeting resolutions.
